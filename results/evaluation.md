# Retrieval evaluation

70 labeled questions over the PostgreSQL 17.0 documentation, 55 of them answerable. Generated by `make eval`; the raw per-question record is in `results/eval_raw.json`.

## How to read these numbers

**Hit@k** — did a correct passage appear in the top *k* results? It is a yes/no per question, averaged over the set.

**MRR** — mean reciprocal rank. Each question scores `1/rank` of its first correct result, so rank 1 scores 1.00 and rank 5 scores 0.20. Hit@k cannot tell rank 1 from rank 3; MRR can.

**Two levels.** All 374 configuration parameters live in one 54,000-token `config.sgml`, and 23 of the 55 answerable questions point at it. *File-level* counts a hit as soon as any chunk from the right file appears — including one about locale settings. *Section-level* asks whether the right passage was found. The gap between them is a measure of how much the file-level number flatters.

Only answerable questions are scored. The 15 unanswerable ones have no gold passage, so every retriever scores zero on them; including them would move all three numbers by the same amount and tell you nothing. Abstention is measured in Phase 6, where it belongs.

On 55 questions, one question is worth 0.018. A gap smaller than that is one question changing rank, not a finding.

## Chunking ablation

The same retrievers over fixed-size chunks, which ignore document structure entirely. This is what justifies Phase 2's complexity — or fails to.

**Only file-level scoring is comparable here.** A fixed chunk has no heading path — that is the definition of fixed chunking — so section-level scoring is not a contest it loses, it is a measurement that does not exist for it. Reporting the structural section-level MRR against a mechanical zero would be a fabricated landslide.

| Retriever | Structural MRR | Fixed MRR | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| BM25 only | 0.739 | 0.669 | +0.070 |
| Dense only | 0.763 | 0.752 | +0.011 |
| Hybrid (RRF) | 0.777 | 0.789 | -0.012 |

- BM25 only: structural is 0.070 ahead of fixed, about 3.8 questions.

What structure-aware chunking buys is not visible in this table. It is the heading path, which is what makes section-level scoring possible at all — and section level is the measurement that reflects whether the right *passage* was found rather than the right file. The ablation shows the two chunkers are close on the coarse metric; the fine metric only exists for one of them.

## Hybrid retrieval did not win, and here is why

The plan expects RRF fusion to beat either retriever alone. On this corpus it does not: dense retrieval scores 0.613 on section-level MRR against the hybrid's 0.585, a gap of 0.028 — about 1.5 questions.

Reporting that plainly is the point. The mechanism is visible in the per-question record (`python -m src.eval.analyze`):

- Against BM25 only, fusion improved 22 questions and worsened 9 — a clear net gain.
- Against Dense only, fusion improved 14 questions and worsened 14 — **net zero**.

RRF cannot invent a good result; it can only reorder what its inputs give it. When dense retrieval already ranks the gold passage first and BM25 ranks it tenth, averaging the two ranks pulls it down. Fusion helps the weaker retriever and costs the stronger one, and on a corpus where one input is consistently better, that trade is not worth making.

The hybrid still wins at file level and on Hit@3 and Hit@5 at section level, so it is not strictly worse — it is better at finding the right area and worse at ranking the exact passage first. Which matters depends on what is downstream. For Phase 6, where roughly five chunks are handed to a generator, Hit@5 is arguably the more relevant number, and there the hybrid leads.

## What these numbers do not show

- **Precision.** A retriever returning the gold passage at rank 1 and noise at 2–5 scores the same as one returning five relevant chunks. Nothing here penalizes the noise.
- **Abstention.** Every number above is computed over answerable questions only. Whether the system knows when to say 'I don't know' is Phase 6.
- **Answer quality.** This measures retrieval, not generation. A perfect Hit@1 still says nothing about whether the answer built from that passage is correct.
- **Sample size.** 55 questions. Differences below 0.018 are one question, and the report says so rather than reporting them as results.
